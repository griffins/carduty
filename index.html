How KRA calculates car import duty in Kenya

This KRA CRSP calculator uses the official CRSP (Current Retail Selling Price) list as the KRA valuation basis for every car. The CRSP is adjusted by a KRA factor and the vehicle's age to arrive at the Customs Value, from which five levies — customs duty, excise duty, VAT, IDF and RDL — are charged. Together these make up the total tax you pay to KRA before your car is released.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate KRA car import duty in Kenya?

Take the car's CRSP value, divide it by the KRA factor of 2.4469, then apply depreciation for the vehicle's age to get the Customs Value (CV). KRA then charges import duty at 25% of CV, excise duty at 20% of (CV + import duty), VAT at 16% of (CV + import duty + excise), IDF at 2.25% of CV (minimum KES 5,000) and RDL at 1.5% of CV. Duty Check does all of this automatically for 5,200+ vehicles.

What is CRSP?

CRSP stands for Current Retail Selling Price — the official price list KRA publishes for every car model. It is the starting value KRA uses to work out the customs value and, from there, the import duty and taxes. This calculator uses the KRA CRSP list dated July 2025.

How much is import duty on a car in Kenya?

There is no single flat figure — the total KRA duty depends on the car's CRSP value and its age. Combined import duty, excise duty, VAT, IDF and RDL typically add up to roughly 60–90% of the customs value. Select your car above to see the exact amount for your model and year.

How old a car can I import into Kenya?

Kenya's 8-year rule means you can only import a vehicle manufactured within the last 8 years. Cars older than that are blocked at customs, so the calculator will flag any year of manufacture that is too old.
